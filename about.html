Why a pharmacist became a loan officer.

For a quarter century, Diane Pepe stood between people and some of the most important decisions in their lives. As a pharmacist, her job was to listen, check the details nobody else had time for, and make sure every person walked away understanding exactly what they had and how to use it.

A mortgage, it turns out, asks for the same things. It’s a long-term commitment with fine print that matters. The right choice depends on the person, not the product. And most people are never given the time to actually understand it.

Twenty-five years. Seen it all. Still believes every person deserves someone who slows down, explains it, and gets it right.

So Diane made mortgage lending her second career. She isn’t here to conquer the world or chase volume. She wants to build something meaningful in her own community — helping neighbors in the Denver Metro area and Lexington, Nebraska buy, refinance, and plan with confidence.

What working with Diane feels like.

Clients describe her the same way: straightforward, patient, and always available. You’ll get a clear checklist of what she needs, honest answers about what you qualify for, and regular updates so you’re never wondering where things stand.

Her approach is educational and friendly, never salesy. Whether you’re a first-time buyer worried about your credit score, a veteran using an earned benefit, or a homeowner wondering whether refinancing makes sense, she’ll lay out the options in plain language and help you choose.

Who Diane helps.

Young professionals and students buying a first place. Parents and growing families. Small business owners and entrepreneurs with income that doesn’t fit a simple box. Gen Z and millennial buyers entering the market. Seniors downsizing into their next chapter. And the real estate agents who want a lender they can count on.

Principles of practice

What 25 years in pharmacy taught her.

  • I.

    Check twice.

    Details caught early are problems that never happen. Diane reviews your file before underwriting does.

  • II.

    Explain plainly.

    If you don’t understand your loan, it isn’t the right loan yet. Every question is a good one.

  • III.

    Fit the person.

    The best program is the one that matches your life, budget, and plans — not a one-size-fits-all product.

Buying a home

How much do I need for a down payment?

Many buyers need far less than 20%. Some conventional loans allow 3% down for qualifying first-time buyers, FHA loans allow 3.5% with a credit score of 580 or higher, and VA loans often require no down payment for eligible veterans. Down payment assistance may also be available in Colorado and Nebraska depending on your income and location.

What is the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval?

A pre-qualification is a quick estimate based on information you provide, while a pre-approval means a lender has reviewed your credit, income and assets. Sellers and agents take pre-approvals more seriously, so Diane recommends getting pre-approved before you start touring homes.

What documents do I need to get pre-approved?

Most buyers need a photo ID, about 30 days of recent pay stubs, two years of W-2s, and two months of bank statements. Self-employed buyers usually also need two years of personal and business tax returns. Diane will send a simple checklist tailored to your situation.

How long does it take to close on a house?

Many purchase loans close in roughly 30 to 45 days after an accepted offer. The timeline depends on the loan program, the appraisal, the property, and how quickly documents come in, which is why Diane gives every client a clear checklist up front.

What closing costs should I expect?

Closing costs commonly include lender fees, appraisal, title insurance, recording fees, and prepaid items such as property taxes and homeowners insurance. Your Loan Estimate shows the exact figures, and in some cases seller credits or assistance programs can help cover them.

Credit and qualifying

What credit score do I need to buy a house?

It depends on the program. FHA loans allow a 3.5% down payment with a score of 580 or higher, and conventional loans generally require at least 620. VA loans have no single official minimum, though lenders set their own guidelines. A higher score usually means a better rate.

Can I buy a home with less-than-perfect credit?

Often, yes. Programs like FHA are designed for borrowers who are still rebuilding credit. If you aren't ready yet, Diane will give you a specific plan, such as which balances to pay down, to help you qualify.

Will shopping for a mortgage hurt my credit score?

Only slightly. Credit scoring models treat multiple mortgage inquiries within a short shopping window as a single inquiry, so you can compare lenders without a large impact on your score.

Can I qualify if I'm self-employed or own a small business?

Yes. Self-employed borrowers typically qualify using two years of tax returns, and Diane will review how your business income is calculated so there are no surprises in underwriting.

Rates and refinancing

Should I wait for mortgage rates to drop before buying?

Not necessarily. Waiting can mean paying a higher price or competing with more buyers when rates fall. Many buyers purchase when the payment fits their budget and refinance later if rates drop enough to make it worthwhile. Diane can show you both scenarios with real numbers.

When does refinancing make sense?

Refinancing usually makes sense when the savings from a lower rate or the benefit of removing mortgage insurance outweigh the closing costs within a reasonable time. Diane calculates your break-even point so you can decide with clear numbers.

What is a rate buy-down?

A rate buy-down is an upfront payment, sometimes covered by the seller or builder, that lowers your interest rate either temporarily or for the life of the loan. It can make the first years of homeownership more affordable.

Colorado and Nebraska

Are there down payment assistance programs in Colorado?

Yes. Colorado has state and local down payment assistance programs, including options through the Colorado Housing and Finance Authority (CHFA). Eligibility depends on income, purchase price, and location, so Diane will check which programs you may qualify for.

Are there first-time buyer programs in Nebraska?

Yes. The Nebraska Investment Finance Authority (NIFA) and some local organizations offer programs for qualifying buyers. Diane can help buyers in Lexington and Dawson County understand which options may apply.

Can Diane help me if I'm relocating to Denver or Lexington?

Yes. Diane helps buyers relocating for work or family coordinate their loan timeline with a move date, a home sale in another state, and a new job, and she can connect you with trusted local real estate agents.
